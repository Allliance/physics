\subsubsection*{Solution}
\paragraph*{Step-by-Step Derivation}

We use the standard cell-autonomous interpretation: along a lineage, the semi-Markov growth-rate process continues independently of division, while descendant branches subsequently evolve independently. Division noise is independent of the growth-rate process. Gamma-driven telegraph processes are naturally treated as alternating renewal processes; their moment-generating functions can be obtained using renewal equations \href{\detokenize{https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/EBD1A21E96B138EDF54FDE0275B7B069/S0001867821000549a.pdf/some_results_on_the_telegraph_process_driven_by_gamma_components.pdf}}{Martinucci, Meoli, and Zacks}. Size regulation correlates successive generation times, so a naive uncorrelated Euler-Lotka equation is not appropriate \href{\detokenize{https://journals.aps.org/pre/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevE.103.L060402}}{Pigolotti, 2021}.

\subparagraph*{1. Size dynamics and homeostasis}

After symmetric division, the next birth size is

\[
v_{b,n+1}=\frac{v_{d,n}}{2} =v_{b,n}^{1-\beta}\bar v_b^\beta+\frac{\xi_n}{2}.
\]

Define

\[
x_n=\frac{v_{b,n}}{\bar v_b}, \qquad \epsilon_n=\frac{\xi_n}{2\bar v_b}.
\]

Then

\[
x_{n+1}=x_n^{1-\beta}+\epsilon_n,
\]

with

\[
\left\langle\epsilon_n\right\rangle=0, \qquad \operatorname{Var}(\epsilon_n)=\frac{\sigma^2}{4\bar v_b^2}.
\]

For small fluctuations, write $x_n=1+\delta_n$. To leading order,

\[
\delta_{n+1}=(1-\beta)\delta_n+\epsilon_n+O(\delta_n^2).
\]

Because $0<\beta\leq1$,

\[
|1-\beta|<1,
\]

so the birth-size distribution is stationary. Its leading variance is

\[
\operatorname{Var}(\delta) = \frac{\operatorname{Var}(\epsilon)} {1-(1-\beta)^2} = \frac{\sigma^2} {4\bar v_b^2\beta(2-\beta)} +O\!\left(\frac{\sigma^4}{\bar v_b^4}\right).
\]

Thus the narrow-noise expansion requires more precisely

\[
\frac{\sigma^2} {4\bar v_b^2\beta(2-\beta)}\ll1.
\]

\subparagraph*{2. Why division-size noise does not change the exponent}

Let

\[
A(t)=\int_0^t\lambda_s\,ds
\]

be the accumulated logarithmic single-cell growth, and let $D(t)$ be the number of divisions along a forward lineage.

Since every division halves the size of the selected daughter,

\[
v(t)=v(0)\frac{e^{A(t)}}{2^{D(t)}}.
\]

Therefore,

\[
2^{D(t)} = e^{A(t)}\frac{v(0)}{v(t)}.
\]

For symmetric binary division, the many-to-one identity gives

\[
\mathbb E[N(t)] = \mathbb E_{\mathrm{lin}}\!\left[2^{D(t)}\right].
\]

Consequently,

\[
\mathbb E[N(t)] = v(0)\, \mathbb E_{\mathrm{lin}} \left[ \frac{e^{A(t)}}{v(t)} \right].
\]

Because $\beta>0$ produces a stationary, time-independent cell-size distribution, $v(0)/v(t)$ contributes only a subexponential factor. Hence

\[
\Lambda = \lim_{t\to\infty} \frac{1}{t} \ln\mathbb E\!\left[ e^{\int_0^t\lambda_s\,ds} \right].
\]

Thus $\Lambda$ is the scaled cumulant-generating function of the integrated growth rate at conjugate parameter $q=1$. The size-control rule, $\beta$, $\bar v_b$, and $\sigma^2$ do not enter this exponent. This biomass-population-growth connection for symmetric, size-homeostatic division is also consistent with established cell-size-control results \href{\detokenize{https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cels.2017.08.015}}{Lin and Amir, 2017}.

\subparagraph*{3. Renewal equation for the two-state growth process}

Let $M_\pm(t)$ be

\[
M_\pm(t) = \mathbb E_\pm \left[ e^{\int_0^t\lambda_s\,ds} \right],
\]

when the process begins at the start of a $\pm$ sojourn. Let

\[
\overline F_\pm(t)=1-\int_0^t f_\pm(u)\,du
\]

be the corresponding survival probability.

Conditioning on the first switching time gives

\[
M_+(t) = e^{\lambda^+t}\overline F_+(t) + \int_0^t f_+(u)e^{\lambda^+u}M_-(t-u)\,du,
\]

and similarly,

\[
M_-(t) = e^{\lambda^-t}\overline F_-(t) + \int_0^t f_-(u)e^{\lambda^-u}M_+(t-u)\,du.
\]

Taking a Laplace transform in $t$, the common denominator is

\[
1- \widehat f_+(s-\lambda^+) \widehat f_-(s-\lambda^-).
\]

The dominant real singularity $s=\Lambda$ therefore satisfies

\[
\widehat f_+(\Lambda-\lambda^+) \widehat f_-(\Lambda-\lambda^-)=1.
\]

For the gamma waiting-time density,

\[
\widehat f_\pm(z) = \int_0^\infty e^{-zt}f_\pm(t)\,dt = \left(\frac{k_\pm}{k_\pm+z}\right)^\alpha.
\]

Hence

\[
\left[ \frac{k_+k_-} {\left(k_++\Lambda-\lambda^+\right) \left(k_-+\Lambda-\lambda^-\right)} \right]^\alpha =1.
\]

At the dominant real root all factors are positive. Since $\alpha>0$,

\[
\left(k_++\Lambda-\lambda^+\right) \left(k_-+\Lambda-\lambda^-\right) =k_+k_-.
\]

Expanding gives the quadratic equation

\[
\Lambda^2 + \left(k_++k_- -\lambda^+-\lambda^-\right)\Lambda + (k_+-\lambda^+)(k_--\lambda^-)-k_+k_- =0.
\]

The asymptotic growth rate is the larger root:

\[
\Lambda_0 = \frac{1}{2} \left[ \lambda^++\lambda^--k_+-k_- + \sqrt{ \left(\lambda^+-\lambda^- -k_+ +k_-\right)^2 +4k_+k_- } \right].
\]

The common gamma shape $\alpha$ cancels. It affects transient and correlation properties, but not this dominant exponent. If the two states had different shape parameters, such a cancellation would not generally occur.

\subparagraph*{4. Small division-noise expansion}

Since the division rule enters only through a stationary size factor,

\[
\Lambda = \Lambda_0 + 0\cdot\frac{\sigma^2}{\bar v_b^2} + O\!\left(\frac{\sigma^4}{\bar v_b^4}\right).
\]

In fact, within the stated symmetric, size-homeostatic model, the independence is exact to all algebraic orders. The neglected Gaussian events producing unphysical division sizes are exponentially rare in $\bar v_b^2/\sigma^2$ and are outside the perturbative expansion.

Increasing $\sigma^2$ broadens the cell-size distribution, while increasing $\beta$ strengthens size regulation and reduces that broadening:

\[
\frac{\operatorname{Var}(v_b)}{\bar v_b^2} \simeq \frac{\sigma^2} {4\bar v_b^2\beta(2-\beta)}.
\]

Neither changes the asymptotic population growth rate to first order.
