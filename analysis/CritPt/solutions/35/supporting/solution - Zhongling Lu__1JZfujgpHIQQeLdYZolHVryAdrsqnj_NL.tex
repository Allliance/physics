\subsubsection*{Solution}
\paragraph*{Symmetry-compatible Hamiltonians}

Write the Hermitian ansatz as

\[
H=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{225}c_\alpha P_\alpha,
\qquad c_\alpha\in\mathbb R,
\]

where the $P_\alpha$ are the listed Pauli strings. Introduce the
Jordan--Wigner Majoranas $a_r=A_r$ and $b_r=B_r$. They satisfy

\[
\{a_r,a_s\}=\{b_r,b_s\}=2\delta_{rs},
\qquad \{a_r,b_s\}=0.
\]

Reducing $[H,O_1]=[H,O_2]=0$ with the Pauli algebra gives a
13-dimensional real null space. A convenient basis consists of

\[
K_r=\sech(1)(Z_r+Z_{r+1})+X_rX_{r+1}+Y_rY_{r+1}
-\tanh(1)(X_rY_{r+1}+Y_rX_{r+1}),
\]

for $r=0,\ldots,10$, together with

\[
L_X=X_{10}Z_{11}-\cosh(1)X_{11}-\sinh(1)Y_{11},
\]

\[
L_Y=Y_{10}Z_{11}-\sinh(1)X_{11}-\cosh(1)Y_{11}.
\]

Hence every symmetry-compatible Hamiltonian in the prescribed ansatz is

\[
H=\sum_{r=0}^{10}J_rK_r+\lambda_XL_X+\lambda_YL_Y.
\]

The required normalization fixes $J_0=1$, because the coefficient of
$Y_0Y_1$ in $K_0$ is one.

\paragraph*{Use of the partial amplitudes}

Although the table does not give all $4096$ amplitudes, it contains exactly
the amplitudes needed to evaluate the eigenvalue equation on the 20 rows
$b=0,\ldots,19$. For any listed one- or two-site Pauli string $P_\alpha$,

\[
\langle b|P_\alpha|\psi\rangle
=\eta_\alpha(b)\,\psi_{b\mathbin{\mathrm{xor}}m_\alpha},
\]

where $m_\alpha$ flips the bits acted on by $X$ or $Y$, and
$\eta_\alpha(b)\in\{\pm1,\pm i\}$. Every required amplitude on the right is
present in the supplied table for each of these 20 rows. Site 0 is the
leftmost, most-significant bit of the displayed 12-bit strings.

Substitution into

\[
\langle b|(H-E)|\psi\rangle=0,
\qquad b=0,\ldots,19,
\]

therefore gives 20 complex linear equations for the 13 remaining real
unknowns $J_1,\ldots,J_{10},\lambda_X,\lambda_Y,E$. The real system has full
column rank 13, so the requested normalized Hamiltonian is uniquely fixed by
the partial data.

An unrestricted least-squares solve gives

\[
\lambda_X=-7.2\times10^{-9},
\qquad
\lambda_Y=2.5\times10^{-8},
\]

which are numerical reconstruction noise. Setting these coefficients to zero
and refitting gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
(J_0,\ldots,J_5)&=(1.0000000000,\ 0.9386591485,\ 1.0027396990,\
1.1141144481,\ 0.9800671234,\ 0.9479031767),\\
(J_6,\ldots,J_{10})&=(0.8625373142,\ 1.0476183427,\ 1.1166616336,\
1.0616533227,\ 1.0569949950).
\end{aligned}
\]

The fitted eigenvalue is $E=0.9980670429$. On the 20 closed rows, the
Euclidean residual is $2.53\times10^{-7}$ and the largest complex row residual
is $1.33\times10^{-7}$, consistent with the numerical precision of the
supplied state.

\paragraph*{Expansion in the requested order}

The 225-vector returned by \texttt{answer.py} is the direct expansion of the
Hamiltonian above. Explicitly, its only nonzero entries are

\[
\begin{aligned}
c(Z_0)&=\sech(1)J_0,\\
c(Z_r)&=\sech(1)(J_{r-1}+J_r), &&1\le r\le10,\\
c(Z_{11})&=\sech(1)J_{10},\\
c(X_rX_{r+1})=c(Y_rY_{r+1})&=J_r, &&0\le r\le10,\\
c(X_rY_{r+1})=c(Y_rX_{r+1})&=-\tanh(1)J_r, &&0\le r\le10.
\end{aligned}
\]

All other one-site, adjacent-pair, and distance-two coefficients vanish.
Because every $K_r$ commutes with both symmetries, the two requested normalized
commutator norms are zero in exact arithmetic.
