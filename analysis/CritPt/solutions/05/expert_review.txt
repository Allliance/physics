Question: Solution review notes
Answer:
1. This question is more mathematical than physical. Although the Marchenko–Pastur distribution originates in random-matrix theory and has many applications in physics, the task itself is the evaluation of a parameter derivative of a hypergeometric function.

2. The problem is standard in the literature, and the same logarithmic moment can be found in multiple references. In particular, it is treated in the Mathematics Stack Exchange discussion [*Integration over the Marchenko-Pastur distribution*](https://math.stackexchange.com/questions/2250884/integration-over-the-marchenko-pastur-distribution), where the hypergeometric differential equation is used.

3. The presented final answer has been verified to be correct. The step-by-step solution has also been checked and is mathematically correct. Its path is logically direct: it applies Euler's integral representation, differentiates under the integral sign, and evaluates the resulting logarithmic integral. However, this route is long and requires a substantial amount of algebra and integral computation.

4. There is a shorter derivation. A quadratic transformation simplifies the target function to
\[
   f(n,\alpha)={}_2F_1(1-n,-n;2;\alpha).
\]
Its Gauss series can be differentiated termwise, and the resulting elementary series can be summed directly. I therefore provide this approach as an alternative solution. One could instead adopt the differential-equation route described in the Mathematics Stack Exchange answer, provided that the commutation of the \(n\)- and \(\alpha\)-derivatives is justified.
